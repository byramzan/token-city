# House Content System: Pump.fun, Crypto Twitter, Internet Culture, Item Placement, and NPC Dialogue

**Research date:** August 6, 2026  
**Pump.fun observation window:** July 30–August 6, 2026  
**Purpose:** Content and implementation specification for house decorations, weekly meme collections, NPC interactions, dialogue bubbles, safe item placement, and texture performance

---

## 1. Product Goal

The game should turn current crypto culture into a living, humorous layer of the city without becoming a token advertisement or a financial dashboard.

House content must be divided into three layers:

1. **Permanent crypto culture**  
   Long-lived language and jokes such as GM, trenches, ape in, rug, bags, diamond hands, cope, WAGMI, and exit liquidity.

2. **Weekly Pump.fun culture**  
   Fast-changing narratives, naming patterns, visual themes, and community behavior observed during the current week.

3. **Pop-culture and internet culture**  
   Original parody objects inspired by recognizable genres, online behavior, retro technology, games, science fiction, AI culture, and classic meme formats.

Real token names, logos, contract addresses, and promotional claims must not be inserted automatically. Current projects should inspire original parody archetypes.

Examples:

- Strategic Meme Reserve;
- Department of Extremely Serious Assets;
- Tokenized Nothing Fund;
- Official Unofficial Oil Barrel;
- Four-Letter Ticker Generator;
- Definitely Real Community Office.

This approach keeps the content topical while reducing legal, moderation, reputational, and financial-promotion risks.

Pump.fun rankings change continuously. Young-token market caps can move sharply, and repeated tickers, low liquidity, and short bursts of activity mean that displayed market cap alone is not proof of durable popularity.

Weekly content selection must consider:

- project age of seven days or less;
- position in the official ranking;
- number of traders;
- transaction volume;
- whether multiple projects share the same narrative;
- whether the underlying idea is discussed beyond one token;
- manual review for scams, prohibited content, impersonation, and intellectual-property issues.

This research is for cultural content design, not investment analysis.

## 3. Weekly Trend Collection System

### 3.1. Release model

Once per week, a content editor selects one dominant Pump.fun narrative and creates a temporary collection of 5–8 objects.

The collection remains obtainable for seven days. After the rotation:

- it stops being granted to new users;
- it remains inside houses where it was already installed;
- it receives a date label;
- it becomes part of the in-game Meme History Archive;
- its dialogue set remains available only near installed collection items.

### 3.2. Required data for every collection

- collection ID;
- display name;
- active date range;
- source summary;
- visual theme;
- item list;
- interaction animations;
- dialogue IDs;
- compatible houses and slots;
- rarity;
- content-review status;
- rights-review status;
- blocked names and images;
- performance budget;
- fallback assets.

### 3.3. Prohibited behavior

The system must not:

- show a button to buy a real token;
- promise price growth;
- repeat unverified claims from a project;
- copy a logo without permission;
- show a contract address as decoration;
- present a Pump.fun trend as a recommendation;
- ingest popular tokens automatically without moderation;
- load images directly from user-supplied or token-metadata URLs.

## 8. Item Placement Architecture

### 8.1. Core rule

Items must never be placed freely in arbitrary 3D space in the production game.

Every item must be installed into an authored and validated placement socket. This prevents:

- objects clipping through walls;
- floating objects;
- furniture intersecting other furniture;
- blocked doors;
- broken NPC paths;
- objects appearing inside windows;
- inconsistent scale;
- incorrect rotation;
- different results across devices.

The user may choose an item and a compatible slot, but may not manually translate, rotate, or scale it.

### 8.2. Coordinate convention

All house, room, slot, item, and animation assets must use one convention:

- 1 engine unit equals 1 meter;
- positive Y is up;
- the item’s forward direction is positive Z;
- the pivot is centered horizontally at the item’s lowest contact point;
- rotations are authored in the asset and saved in degrees or radians consistently across the project;
- non-uniform runtime scaling is prohibited;
- transforms must be frozen before export.

No asset may depend on a hidden parent scale or an arbitrary import correction.

### 8.3. Socket types

Supported socket types:

- `floor_small`;
- `floor_medium`;
- `floor_large`;
- `wall_small`;
- `wall_medium`;
- `wall_large`;
- `tabletop`;
- `shelf`;
- `ceiling`;
- `window_sill`;
- `door_side`;
- `outdoor_ground`;
- `outdoor_wall`;
- `interactive_station`.

Every socket stores:

- unique socket ID;
- house variant ID;
- room ID;
- socket type;
- position;
- rotation;
- maximum width, depth, and height;
- allowed item categories;
- blocked item tags;
- required clearance;
- interaction direction;
- NPC standing point;
- camera focus point;
- occupancy state.

### 8.4. Required item metadata

Every item definition must include:

- item ID;
- display name;
- content collection;
- category;
- rarity;
- compatible socket types;
- width, depth, and height;
- collision bounds;
- clearance bounds;
- material tags;
- room tags;
- outdoor or indoor restriction;
- default rotation;
- interaction type;
- NPC animation ID;
- NPC approach anchor;
- camera focus anchor;
- dialogue tags;
- model asset;
- texture assets;
- LOD assets;
- low-quality fallback asset;
- performance cost;
- rights-review status;
- moderation status.

An item with incomplete placement or performance metadata must not be published.

### 8.5. Bounds

Each item requires two separate bounds:

1. **Collision bounds**  
   The physical volume occupied by the visible object.

2. **Clearance bounds**  
   Additional empty space required for doors, drawers, animated parts, NPCs, and camera readability.

Examples:

- a chair needs rear clearance for an NPC animation;
- a cabinet needs front clearance for its doors;
- a wall poster needs almost no depth but must not overlap a window;
- a Copium Tank needs side clearance for its particle effect;
- a telescope needs an unobstructed viewing direction.

### 8.6. Minimum clearances

Recommended baseline values:

- at least 0.8 m for primary NPC walking routes;
- at least 0.6 m in front of doors;
- at least 0.5 m around an interaction point;
- at least 0.15 m below the ceiling;
- at least 0.1 m from a window frame;
- at least 0.05 m between decorative objects;
- no item clearance volume may overlap an emergency fallback path.

These values may be adjusted for the visual scale, but one shared rule set must be used across all houses.

### 8.7. Compatibility rules

Compatibility is determined by data, not by object names.

Examples:

- a wall poster requires a wall socket;
- the MEV Sandwich Toaster requires a tabletop socket;
- the Wen Moon Telescope requires a window or outdoor socket;
- the Rug Trap requires a floor socket outside the main NPC route;
- the Bull Market Bust requires a medium tabletop, shelf, or pedestal socket;
- the Exit Liquidity Fountain is outdoor only;
- a ceiling lamp cannot be installed in a room with insufficient height.

Material-based collections may influence recommendations, but should not create hard restrictions unless the asset is visually incompatible.

### 8.8. Placement validation order

Before an item appears in the final house, validate:

1. The item exists and is enabled.
2. The user owns or has unlocked it.
3. The socket belongs to the user’s house.
4. The socket is empty.
5. The socket type is compatible.
6. Item dimensions fit the socket.
7. Collision bounds do not overlap architecture.
8. Clearance bounds do not overlap doors, windows, other items, or NPC paths.
9. The interaction anchor is reachable.
10. Required model, texture, LOD, and fallback assets exist.
11. The placement remains within the room’s allowed bounds.
12. The protected side confirms the final placement.

The client preview is not authoritative. Placement must be validated again before saving.

### 8.9. User preview

During item selection:

- show a semi-transparent preview in the selected socket;
- use a green outline for a valid placement;
- use a red outline for an invalid placement;
- show a short reason when placement fails;
- do not let the user drag the object away from the socket;
- update price and ownership state before confirmation;
- preserve the previous installed item until the new placement is confirmed.

### 8.10. Atomic slot updates

Replacing an object must be a single atomic operation:

1. Validate the new item.
2. Reserve the socket.
3. Save the new item assignment.
4. Release the old item back to inventory.
5. Confirm the final state.

If any step fails, the old item remains installed. A failure must never leave two items in one socket or delete the previous item.

## 10. Content-Authoring Pipeline

Every new object must pass through the following process.

### Step 1. Content proposal

Define:

- joke;
- visual silhouette;
- target room;
- interaction;
- dialogue tags;
- collection;
- rights risk;
- expected rarity.

### Step 2. Low-poly model

Requirements:

- correct meter scale;
- frozen transforms;
- correct pivot;
- clean normals;
- no hidden geometry;
- no unnecessary internal faces;
- no tiny geometry that disappears at gameplay distance;
- no non-manifold geometry;
- limited material slots.

### Step 3. Socket test

Test the object in every supported socket and house variant.

An object cannot declare compatibility with a socket type unless it has been visually checked in all house layouts containing that socket type.

### Step 4. Collision and clearance

Create collision and clearance bounds. Test:

- walls;
- floor;
- ceiling;
- windows;
- doors;
- adjacent items;
- resident paths;
- interaction animations.

### Step 5. NPC interaction

For interactive objects, define:

- approach point;
- facing direction;
- interaction animation;
- maximum simultaneous users;
- interaction duration;
- exit direction;
- fallback idle animation.

If the interaction animation is missing, the NPC must use the fallback idle behavior rather than entering the object.

### Step 6. Camera and dialogue

Define:

- camera focus point;
- minimum useful viewing distance;
- speech-bubble anchor;
- dialogue tags;
- occlusion behavior.

Dialogue bubbles must attach to the NPC, not to the object’s origin.

### Step 7. Performance validation

Validate:

- polygon count;
- material count;
- texture resolution;
- texture format;
- transparency;
- shader type;
- LOD;
- loading time;
- GPU upload behavior;
- mobile fallback.

### Step 8. Device test

Test at minimum:

- desktop high quality;
- desktop low quality;
- modern iPhone or equivalent;
- mid-range Android device;
- throttled network;
- cold cache;
- warm cache;
- house containing the maximum allowed number of objects.

### Step 9. Controlled release

Release behind a content flag. If errors are observed, disable the object without deleting it from user inventories.

## 11. Missing-Asset and Failure Behavior

The game must never show:

- a pink missing-material model;
- a black texture;
- an invisible interactive object;
- an item floating at world origin;
- an NPC interacting with empty space;
- a broken image icon;
- an infinite loading state.

Fallback behavior:

- missing model: show a neutral low-poly delivery box;
- missing texture: use the item’s declared solid fallback color;
- missing animation: use idle inspection animation;
- missing dialogue: use a generic contextual line;
- invalid socket: return the item to inventory;
- failed save: keep the previously installed object;
- failed weekly asset: disable the item remotely and preserve ownership.

Every asset-loading error should log:

- asset ID;
- house ID;
- socket ID;
- device-quality level;
- format;
- loading duration;
- failure stage;
- fallback used.

## 12. Current Texture-Lag Problem

### 12.1. Required audit

The current version has visible lag associated with some textures. Before adding more content, create a complete texture inventory.

For every texture record:

- asset ID;
- source model;
- file format;
- pixel dimensions;
- file size;
- estimated GPU memory;
- alpha channel usage;
- mipmap availability;
- compression state;
- number of objects using it;
- whether it is loaded at startup;
- whether it causes a visible frame hitch;
- whether a lower-quality version exists.

Do not solve the problem only by reducing global quality. Identify the specific assets and loading stages causing the stalls.

### 12.2. Common causes to investigate

- 2K or 4K textures on small props;
- uncompressed PNG files;
- unique textures for objects that could share an atlas;
- too many unique material instances;
- shader compilation during the first camera approach;
- textures uploaded to the GPU during active camera movement;
- missing mipmaps;
- excessive anisotropic filtering;
- large transparent surfaces;
- alpha blending where alpha cutout would be sufficient;
- multiple overlapping emissive materials;
- runtime-generated canvas textures;
- remote token images loaded from unpredictable servers;
- textures being repeatedly unloaded and reloaded;
- interior textures loading together with the whole city;
- high-resolution normal maps with little visible benefit;
- different color variants stored as separate full textures.

### 12.3. Texture resolution rules

Recommended maximums:

- tiny props: 128–256 px;
- normal decorative props: 256–512 px;
- large hero props: up to 1024 px;
- house material atlases: up to 1024 or 2048 px where justified;
- large environment atlas: 2048 px maximum for MVP;
- 4K textures: prohibited unless separately approved and proven necessary.

Resolution must be based on on-screen size, not on the size of the source artwork.

### 12.4. Texture formats

Preferred runtime format:

- KTX2/Basis compressed textures for supported 3D content;
- WebP or AVIF for suitable UI and non-GPU-specialized images;
- PNG only when lossless alpha is genuinely required;
- JPEG only for non-transparent photographic or baked imagery.

Provide a tested fallback where compressed GPU texture support is unavailable.

All project textures must be processed and hosted by the project. Do not load arbitrary images directly from token metadata or user URLs.

### 12.5. Atlases and reuse

- Reuse the same wood, brick, stone, metal, and glass material instances.
- Put small weekly-object labels into an approved texture atlas.
- Use one shared decal atlas for fictional stamps, tickers, signs, and certificates.
- Prefer material parameters or vertex colors for palette variants.
- Do not create a separate full-resolution texture only to change one color.
- Repeated objects must reference the same texture resource in memory.

### 12.6. Material limits

Recommended limits:

- small prop: 1 material;
- medium interactive object: up to 2 materials;
- large signature object: up to 3 materials with approval;
- normal house exterior: shared material set, not unique materials per wall;
- transparent materials: only when visually necessary.

Every extra material can increase draw calls and shader work.

### 12.7. Mipmaps and filtering

- Generate mipmaps for textures visible at multiple distances.
- Verify that mipmaps do not create halos around transparent edges.
- Use moderate anisotropic filtering.
- Suggested maximum: 4× on mobile and 8× on desktop.
- Avoid forcing maximum anisotropy globally.
- Use alpha cutout for signs, leaves, or decals when smooth transparency is unnecessary.

### 12.8. Progressive loading

Loading order:

1. Low-resolution city materials.
2. Nearby house exterior materials.
3. Nearby residents and public building.
4. Selected-house high-quality exterior.
5. Interior only after the user requests interior view.
6. High-quality item textures after the room becomes visible.

While a texture is loading:

- show the declared solid fallback color or low-resolution texture;
- do not freeze the camera;
- do not delay the whole city;
- fade to the final texture only after upload is complete.

### 12.9. Shader prewarming

Frequently used shaders should be prepared before the player approaches a detailed house.

Prewarm:

- common house materials;
- transparent window material;
- emissive sign material;
- low-poly resident material;
- dialogue-bubble material;
- weekly object material;
- selected-object outline.

Shader compilation should not occur for the first time while the camera is rapidly moving toward a house.

### 12.10. Texture memory budgets

Use configurable budgets for the currently active zone.

Initial targets:

- mobile: approximately 128 MB for active 3D textures;
- desktop low/medium: approximately 192 MB;
- desktop high: approximately 256 MB.

These are starting targets and must be validated on real devices. When the budget is approached:

- reduce texture mip level;
- unload interiors that are no longer visible;
- unload distant weekly objects;
- preserve shared city materials;
- never unload a texture that is still referenced by a visible object.

### 12.11. Cache behavior

- Cache shared textures by stable asset ID.
- Use reference counting or an equivalent ownership mechanism.
- Do not decode the same image multiple times for identical objects.
- Keep common house materials warm while moving between nearby houses.
- Unload selected-house interior assets after a controlled delay.
- Cancel obsolete loads when the user quickly changes the selected house.

### 12.12. Low-quality mode

Low-quality mode should:

- use smaller texture variants;
- reduce anisotropy;
- disable nonessential normal maps;
- reduce emissive effects;
- reduce transparent overlays;
- show simplified weekly objects;
- keep text and main silhouettes readable.

Low-quality mode must not replace all materials with visually unrelated colors.

### 12.13. Texture performance acceptance criteria

- No small prop may use an unapproved 2K or 4K texture.
- Entering a house must not freeze camera movement.
- Loading an item must not stall the main thread for a visibly disruptive period.
- Repeated instances must reuse texture and material resources.
- Texture failure must produce a valid fallback.
- Interior textures must not load during initial city startup.
- Cold-cache and throttled-network behavior must remain usable.
- The maximum-content house must stay within the target memory budget.
- Current problematic textures must be identified in an asset report before the next content release.
- Performance regressions must block publication of the affected item.

## 13. Dialogue-Bubble System

### 13.1. General requirements

- Dialogue remains in English.
- One conversation contains 2–3 short lines.
- One line should not exceed 42 characters.
- No more than two conversations are visible simultaneously.
- Only residents near the camera display bubbles.
- Conversations occur every 12–25 seconds.
- The same dialogue cannot repeat for at least ten minutes.
- Dialogue depends on time, location, item, building, and NPC behavior.
- NPCs should physically interact with the referenced object when possible.
- Humor should use self-irony, literal interpretations of slang, and serious language applied to mundane situations.

### 13.2. Dialogue categories

- greetings;
- domestic conversation;
- café and shop conversation;
- current Pump.fun narrative;
- charts and market behavior;
- CTO and developer jokes;
- object-specific jokes;
- connected-house jokes;
- night dialogue;
- rare pop-culture dialogue.

### 13.3. Frequency weights

- 60% ordinary contextual dialogue;
- 25% crypto-native slang;
- 10% object or building joke;
- 5% weekly trend or pop-culture line.

### 13.4. Bubble placement

Each NPC rig requires a bubble anchor above the head.

The bubble system must:

- project the anchor into screen space;
- avoid overlapping the main HUD;
- avoid covering the selected house;
- offset overlapping bubbles;
- hide bubbles behind the camera;
- fade bubbles when the NPC is occluded for too long;
- hide bubbles beyond the configured distance;
- prevent bubbles from attaching to the item origin.

## 18. Acceptance Criteria

### Content

1. The game contains at least 20 permanent crypto-culture objects.
2. Every main house material has a dedicated thematic object set.
3. Weekly collections can be added without changing core 3D logic.
4. Real tokens are not promoted automatically.
5. Every object passes manual content and rights review.
6. Pop-culture items use original parody forms.
7. The game contains at least 60 dialogue conversations.
8. Dialogue depends on location, time, object, and activity.
9. Dialogue does not repeat excessively.
10. No more than two conversations appear simultaneously.
11. Friendly mode removes disputed terminology.

### Placement

12. Every object uses a validated socket.
13. No production item supports unrestricted free placement.
14. Every object has collision and clearance bounds.
15. Objects cannot block doors, windows, or primary NPC routes.
16. Interactive objects have reachable NPC anchors.
17. Replacing an installed item is atomic.
18. Failed placement preserves the previous installed item.
19. Missing assets always show a valid fallback.
20. Every supported house and socket combination is visually tested.

### Performance

21. Every item has a declared texture and material budget.
22. Small props do not use unapproved high-resolution textures.
23. Shared objects reuse textures and materials.
24. Interiors do not load during initial city startup.
25. Texture loading does not freeze camera movement.
26. Current lagging textures are identified in a written asset audit.
27. Mobile and desktop quality levels use appropriate texture variants.
28. Missing or failed textures never display as black, pink, or invisible.
29. The maximum-content house remains within the active texture-memory target.
30. Performance regressions block the affected item from release.
