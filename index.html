Family Life and the Player-Owned Economy

The game is a shared town built by its players. Each player owns one family home and may choose to purchase a business. Families need food, rest, learning, and time together. Businesses provide the goods and experiences that help them thrive.

This creates a simple economic loop: families visit businesses owned by other players, business owners earn Game Coins from real customers, and those coins continue circulating through the town.

Your Home and Family

Every home belongs to one family. A one-floor house begins with four residents. Players can choose up to five floors when building their home or add floors later. Each additional floor adds two family members:

More floors give the family more space and create greater demand for food, books, gifts, and services. They do not create free income. A five-floor home is still one family, not five separate households.

Larger families need larger purchases to receive the full benefit. For example, a meal for four will only partly satisfy a family of eight. Businesses can offer family-size packages so players do not have to make many small purchases.

The Four Family Needs

Every family has four shared needs, each shown on a scale from 0 to 100:

  • Hunger: How well-fed the family is.
  • Energy: How rested and active the family feels.
  • Knowledge: How engaged the family is in reading and learning.
  • Family Bond: How connected family members feel to one another.

Needs decrease gradually during active play. Hunger is the fastest: without food, it takes approximately eight active hours to reach zero from a full bar. Energy takes approximately ten hours, Knowledge fourteen, and Family Bond eighteen. These are starting balance targets and may be adjusted as the game develops.

Needs decrease more slowly when the browser is in the background or the player is offline. A player should not return after a long absence to find that the entire family has suddenly entered a crisis.

The game warns players before a need reaches zero and recommends nearby businesses that can help.

Visiting Businesses

This is a browser game, so players do not need to steer each resident through a shop. They click a business, review its offers, and choose what the family needs. Before confirming, they can see the price, how many family members the offer covers, and the expected effect.

Businesses have limited goods or service capacity. Owners choose what to offer and at what price within the game's rules, then replenish their stock or capacity to keep serving customers. A sale is revenue, not necessarily profit: owners also need to consider the cost of providing what they sell.

What Happens When a Need Reaches Zero

A zero need puts the family into Crisis Focus. The family must address its most urgent problem before it can benefit from unrelated purchases.

For example, if Hunger reaches zero, the family cannot use a book or library visit to restore Knowledge. It must first buy and use food to bring Hunger back to at least 20. Meanwhile, Knowledge and the other needs continue to decrease. Once Hunger reaches 20, normal choices become available again—unless another need has also reached zero.

The same principle applies to every need:

  • At zero Hunger, food is the priority.
  • At zero Energy, rest or an energy-restoring service is the priority.
  • At zero Knowledge, books or learning are the priority.
  • At zero Family Bond, a gift or shared family activity is the priority.

If several needs reach zero, the family addresses them in this order: Hunger, Energy, Knowledge, then Family Bond. If all four reach zero, the game presents those four recovery steps clearly.

A crisis is a temporary restriction, not a punishment that destroys progress. Residents do not disappear. Players do not lose their house, business, furniture, or Game Coins because a need reaches zero.

Player-owned businesses are always presented first. If no suitable player business can provide an essential crisis item or service, a limited city emergency offer prevents the family from becoming permanently stuck. It provides only enough recovery to leave the crisis, costs more than a normal player offer, and does not compete with player businesses during ordinary play.

How Business Owners Earn

A player buys a business separately from their home. When another player purchases its goods or services, the business owner receives the sale proceeds in Game Coins, less the disclosed marketplace fee.

The planned marketplace fee is 5% of a completed sale. For a purchase priced at 100 Game Coins, the seller receives 95 Game Coins and the platform receives 5. The buyer sees the full price before confirming, and the seller sees what they will receive.

Owners can earn by offering useful products at competitive prices, keeping their business supplied, and serving the needs of nearby families. A well-run grocery store may attract families seeking dependable food; a café may appeal to players who want both Energy and a social benefit.

A business does not generate Game Coins simply because it exists. Earnings depend on purchases by other players. No level of income, profit, or token value is guaranteed.

Game Coins and the Project Token

Players acquire the project token in their own wallets and can convert eligible tokens into Game Coins under the conversion terms shown in the game. Game Coins are used to build and expand homes, purchase businesses, and buy goods and services from other players.

Business sales move existing Game Coins from customers to sellers; satisfying a family need does not create new coins. Eligible Game Coins may be converted back into the project token under the withdrawal terms displayed at the time. Conversion amounts can vary with the applicable terms and token market conditions.

The purpose of this economy is to make player activity meaningful. Homes create families, families create demand, and businesses compete to meet it. Adding floors expands a family and its needs, while building a successful business means providing something other players genuinely want to buy.
